# 01. The idea and the format

Step 1 of 7. Costs nothing: Claude Code does it with your links and your numbers. Prompt: [`prompts/generate/gen-00-idea.md`](../prompts/generate/gen-00-idea.md). Output: `BRIEF.md`.

Checked 2026-10-09. Platform rules and data link to their source. "Secondary" marks something read in press or a summary and not on the primary page.

The idea step decides more than any model choice. Start from a format that already has views, and never from "make me a cute character".

For the three worked examples in this repo the idea was one line each time, and each one gave the edit something to count or tick: a pre-flight checklist that the flight in a short film ignores (wing-it), the spins of a boy in a stock clip (spin-count), and the 20 seconds that the CDC handwashing song takes when it is sung twice (handwash-song). Steps 2 to 4 were skipped because the footage and the sound already existed ([05-or-use-existing-footage.md](05-or-use-existing-footage.md)).

## 1. What a format needs

### 1.1 A reference with a number you saw yourself

- **Measure the reference.** Collect 10 to 20 recent videos in one niche that are doing well, and write down the views with the date you saw them. A view count you did not see yourself is a claim.
- **Reduce each one to its action sequence**: what happens in the first second, what repeats, the payoff. Keep the action sequence and change the concept.
- **Track speed, not totals.** If you can look twice, compute views per hour: `(later views - earlier views) / hours between`. A recent upload that gains fast tells you more than an old one with a big total.
- **Distrust grids and income posts.** A 3x3 grid of scenes with a view counter on each tile and no account name is usually a mockup. Most "$X a month" posts are self-reports, often next to a tool affiliate link, and the body of such a post sometimes reports a much smaller figure than its title. Do not make grids like that yourself (section 6).

What gets borrowed differs. A character swap borrows someone else's footage and sound. A useful or kids format borrows a structure: counting, steps, an alphabet, a verse and a chorus.

### 1.2 Two families, and only one needs an edit layer

| | Swap family | Structured family |
|---|---|---|
| The video | a character replaces a person in a clip that already went viral; one shot, 10 to 15 s | a song, a workout, a lesson or a short story with a fixed shape |
| Text on screen | none, or one first-person caption | counters, step cards, timers, word highlights, boards, checklists |
| Rights | someone else's footage, often with real people in it | your own song, script and scenes, or footage you are allowed to edit |

This repo teaches the structured family. The edit is code, so the material needs numbers, steps, words, events or a timer for the code to animate. A swap clip gives it nothing to do.

### 1.3 A fixed skeleton, and a new situation every episode

A series that works has a recognisable face, one signature move, and a new situation each time. Repeat the situation that worked, not the location.

The platform rule points the same way. YouTube monetizes "a series following a set of characters across episodes" when "each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept". It does not monetize "Videos where characters are put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome" ([YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)).

The skeleton repeats. The story inside it has to change.

### 1.4 The reason to rewatch is planned before anything is generated

| Reason | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| A loop | a board fills on the sung numbers and the outro counts it back, so the video closes on itself |
| Utility | step cards you can follow along with, ending on a reason to save |
| An open question | "real or AI?", or "how many did you count?" at the end of a counting video |
| A search | something hidden in each shot that viewers look for |
| A predictable beat | the same reveal, again and again, then a finale |
| A broken plan | a checklist or a plan on screen that the story tears up one item at a time (the wing-it example) |

Image quality is not a reason to rewatch. Write the reason into `BRIEF.md` before you spend anything.

### 1.5 Series hooks that bring people back

- **Votes.** Let viewers choose what happens next, or what the character wears next time.
- **Crossovers and rivalries.** Two recurring characters meet, or compete.
- **One character across many videos.** Children remember characters, so a kids series reuses the same ones.

## 2. The scorecard

Score each candidate format 0 to 2 on rows A to F. Row G is pass or fail. Our rule: ship only if G passes, the total is 8 or more out of 12, and neither B nor C is zero. The threshold is our choice and has not been measured against outcomes.

| Row | Criterion | 2 points if | Why this row |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Audience that pays** | adults in a category with buyers (fitness, food, apps, money), or a client who pays for production | median YouTube RPM is $0.33 for Kids & Teens against about $2.30 across all channels (secondary: [AIR, 300 channels](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/which-youtube-niche-makes-the-most-money-in-2026-ranked-by-real-rpm-and-cpm)); Made for Kids turns off personalized ads ([YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654)) |
| B | **Proven mechanism** | a reference with a view count you saw yourself, plus at least two other channels using the same action sequence | section 1.1 |
| C | **Repeatable skeleton** | the episode fits one sentence, and you can list 30 situations that each have a distinct story | section 1.3. If you cannot list 30, the niche is too narrow |
| D | **A character can carry it** | the format needs one face that recurs, and that face reads at phone size | [02-character.md](02-character.md) |
| E | **Room for the edit layer** | the material has numbers, steps, words, events or a timer | section 1.2 |
| F | **A reason to rewatch or save** | a loop, an open question, a utility or a story you can name in one line | section 1.4 |
| G | **Risk gate** (pass/fail) | every item in section 6 passes | section 6 |

## 3. The process

1. **Pick one niche and collect 10 to 20 recent videos that are doing well**, with views and the date you saw them (section 1.1). Do not scrape platforms you have no right to scrape; links and numbers you saw are enough.
2. **Write each candidate's mechanism in one line**: what happens in the first second, the action sequence, the payoff. Example: "giant number pops, flies into a slot, the board counts back at the end".
3. **Score the top candidates** with section 2 and pick before you see how the next week goes. Freeze the ranking first, and also record the top three by view speed alone, so you can tell later whether your judgement beat the plain numbers.
4. **Design the character** for the winning format ([02-character.md](02-character.md)).
5. **Write a series list**: 10 episode situations now, a path to 30, one recurring gag, the fixed skeleton.
6. **Make several episodes before judging.** One character and about 10 videos is a fair first test.
7. **Read the numbers over the first three days.** Keep the situation that worked and change the location. The question for the test: do viewers want another episode?

## 4. Idea templates

Each template lists the skeleton, what the code layer animates, the evidence where there is any, and the risk. The verdict is for this repo.

**1. Steps or counting song with a mascot**
- Skeleton: 25 to 35 s original song, one action per line, numbers 1 to 5, the outro counts back.
- Code layer: word highlight, a marker hopping from word to word, a number board.
- Evidence: 9 of the 30 most-viewed YouTube videos are children's songs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most-viewed_YouTube_videos)); 57.4% of TikTok videos aimed at children were AI-generated slop in Kapwing's sample ([Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/the-tiktok-ai-slop-report/)), so the space is crowded.
- Money: weak. Made for Kids, low RPM, and product placement conflicts with policy (section 5).
- Verdict: **use it to teach the edit technique.** Keep it brand-free: original song, a learning goal, Made for Kids set correctly. The handwashing example in this repo is this template.

**2. No-gym fitness coach**
- Skeleton: hook, "3 moves", one step card per move (name, reps or hold time, one form cue), save call to action.
- Code layer: counters, step cards, progress bar, timer, muscle map.
- Evidence: in Kapwing's TikTok sample, Fitness was 1.6% AI slop, "almost entirely human-made" ([Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/resources/the-tiktok-ai-slop-report/)). Our reading: an AI coach has little AI competition there.
- Risk: a realistic human needs AI labels (section 6). Exercise cues must be correct.
- Verdict: **the best adult template.** Richest graphic layer, legal product categories (shoes, bottles, apps).

**3. Brand or app mascot, made as a service**
- Skeleton: the mascot shows one feature per episode; the same character appears in onboarding, social posts and ads.
- Code layer: interface callouts, feature cards, end card.
- Evidence: a retailer's own mascot was estimated at about $2.54M from 74 sponsored posts (Kapwing estimate, May 2025 data, [report](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/virtual-influencers-the-most-followed-and-top-earning-ai-celebrities/)).
- Verdict: **the cleanest money path** for developers and founders. You are paid for production, and the fee does not depend on views. Do not sell the offer on a promise of views.

**4. A short-film scene with a plan on top** (the wing-it example)
- Skeleton: 30 s of an open movie or your own animation, cut to 9:16; a plan, checklist or rule set on screen that the story breaks item by item; a score at the end.
- Code layer: chips that tick or cross on story events, a countdown, meters, warnings, a counter, an outro card.
- Evidence: none we can cite; it is the format of this repo's hero example.
- Risk: the footage license. Blender Studio's open movies are CC BY and need a credit line and a link to the license ([05-or-use-existing-footage.md](05-or-use-existing-footage.md#a-blender-open-movie-the-wing-it-example)).
- Verdict: **the cheapest way to a strong edit.** No generation at all, and the story gives the timings.

**5. This week's news explained by a cast of objects**
- Skeleton: 1 to 2 minutes, each party is an object or a fruit, a narrated explainer.
- Code layer: name tags, a timeline, quote cards.
- Risk: real people and companies appear; keep it to commentary.

**6. Object reality-show microdrama**
- Skeleton: 2 to 4 minute episodes, couples, a cliffhanger, viewer votes.
- Code layer: vote overlays, episode cards, a "who is coupled" board.
- Risk: do not borrow an existing TV format or its brand. Invent your own show format.

**7. Absurd recurring character in everyday places**
- Skeleton: 10 to 15 s, one signature move, a new everyday place.
- Risk: the usual method swaps the character into someone else's clip with real people in it. Use motion you own: record yourself and use the clip as a motion reference.
- Verdict: weak fit for this repo, because the format has no on-screen text.

**8. Animal mascot in human situations**
- Risk: YouTube's "same situation ... same outcome" line (section 1.3). Each episode needs its own story.
- Code layer: subtitles only.

**For a first video:** template 2 for adults, template 1 or 4 as an edit demo, template 3 if you want to sell the work.

## 5. Money: what holds up

- **YouTube Partner Program** needs 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 watch hours in 12 months or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days ([YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851)).
- **Made for Kids** turns off personalized ads, comments, the notification bell, end screens, and merchandise. YouTube says this "may result in a decrease in revenue" ([YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654)).
- **"Every scene is an ad slot"** (the character can wear it, hold it, drink it) is a plan for adult audiences, with a clear disclosure. The coach is the example: sneakers, bottles, protein, gyms, fitness apps. The FTC treats virtual influencers as endorsers (secondary: [BakerHostetler](https://www.bakerlaw.com/insights/updated-ftc-endorsement-guides-hold-virtual-influencers-to-actual-standards/)).
- **Keep it out of content for young children.** YouTube limits monetization of kids and family content that is "Heavily promotional: Content that mainly features products, brands and logos (such as toys or food)" ([YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). The YouTube Kids app does not allow paid placements (secondary: [YouTube Kids policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10938174)). A toothpaste, snacks and toys slot list for a toddler song is close to that definition. A kids video in this pipeline has no products in it.
- **Vendor programs.** Higgsfield Earn pays per approved video, with a $2,500 maximum per video ([higgsfield.ai/earn](https://higgsfield.ai/earn)). Many of the most visible "viral AI character" accounts are promoted by the company that sells the generation; read their numbers with that in mind.
- **Most "$X a month" posts are self-reports** (section 1.1).

## 6. Red flags (row G)

**Kids**
- [ ] Set Made for Kids honestly. YouTube counts "songs, stories" and "animated characters or cartoon figures" as signs of kids content, and says "Failure to set your content appropriately may result in consequences on YouTube or have legal consequences under COPPA" ([YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076)). Disney paid $10M over mislabelled videos (secondary: [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/327535)).
- [ ] No products in kids videos (section 5).
- [ ] No AI avatar that could make a child think it is a real person in child-directed ads (secondary: [CARU warning](https://bbbprograms.org/media/newsroom/press/caru-compliance-warning-ai)).

**Mass production**
- [ ] Each episode has its own story. YouTube does not monetize "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production" ([YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)).
- [ ] Do not post the same file to many channels. A Google research paper describes a system that terminated about 130,000 channels in clusters for reusing one template; the paper does not name the platform (secondary: [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/artificial-intelligence/how-google-machine-terminated-130000-ai-slop-youtube-channels-six-months-903645)).

**AI labels, per platform**
- [ ] YouTube: disclose photorealistic AI content and AI music that is "the main focus of the video". A fully animated video needs no visual label. A generated song that is the main focus still needs the disclosure. AI-written scripts do not ([YouTube](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)).
- [ ] Instagram: an account whose central identity is an AI person gets an "AI-generated profile" label since 2026-08-31; unlabelled ones may lose reach (secondary: [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/tech/instagram-ai-generated-profile-label-reach-restrictions)).
- [ ] TikTok: label realistic AI content (secondary: [Cinerads](https://www.cinerads.com/blog/tiktok-ai-content-policy)).
- [ ] EU: AI Act Article 50 labelling applies from 2026-08-02 (secondary: [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/07/31/the-new-ai-labelling-rules-for-deployers-in-the-advertising-supply-chain)).

**Advertising honesty**
- [ ] No testimonials from people who do not exist, and no fake view counts or follower counts. The FTC rule bans testimonials "from someone who does not exist" and fake social-media indicators (secondary: [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)).
- [ ] If you show a grid of your scenes, do not put view counters on it that you did not measure.

**IP and likeness**
- [ ] Original character: no real people, no existing characters.
- [ ] No studio or franchise names in prompts ([Picsart persona skill](https://github.com/PicsArt/gen-ai-skills/blob/main/skills/gen-ai-persona-creation/SKILL.md), MIT).
- [ ] No borrowed TV format or brand (section 4, template 6).
- [ ] No real person's footage without permission. For motion, record yourself and use the clip as a motion reference.
